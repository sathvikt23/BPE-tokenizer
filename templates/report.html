🧹 Data Cleaning Pipeline

Every document passes through 10 rigorous cleaning stages before it enters the training pool.

1
Language Identification
Detect and tag the language of each document
2
Exact & Near Deduplication
Remove duplicate and near-identical documents
3
Text Extraction & Normalization
Strip HTML, normalize Unicode and whitespace
4
Heuristic Quality Filtering
Remove spam, SEO junk, and low-signal text using rule-based filters
5
ML-Based Quality Classification
Classify documents by quality using trained classifiers
6
Custom Task Filtering
Apply domain-specific filters (e.g., valid code, valid math)
7
PII Detection & Anonymization
Identify and redact personally identifiable information
8
Domain Classification & Clustering
Assign each document to its data pool (D1–D4, AON)
9
Human Quality Evaluation
Spot-check a sample for final quality assurance
✓
Final Clean Corpus
Ready for tokenizer training and pre-training

Why Organize by Domain, Not Language?

1
Domain expertise over language separation
Programming, math, and science depend on high-quality domain content more than the language it's written in. Separating by language would starve domains of cross-lingual signal.
2
Global + Regional knowledge coexists
Instead of isolated language buckets, regional knowledge (news, govt, education) appears naturally alongside global knowledge in every domain pool.
3
Better cross-lingual learning
Real documents mix English + Hindi, English + Telugu. Training on naturally multilingual documents teaches semantic relationships across languages without dedicated alignment data.
4
Balanced knowledge for all languages
Every language gets exposure to Science, Government, and Education instead of being pigeonholed. This avoids English → Science, Language X → Culture asymmetry.
02 · Scaling Strategy

Chinchilla-Inspired Progressive Scaling

Training efficiency requires balancing model parameters, training tokens, and compute budget. We don't just maximize parameter count — we co-optimize all three.

📊 Chinchilla Insight: More Data > More Parameters
Model Parameters Training Tokens Winner?
GPT-3 175B 300B Under-trained on data
Chinchilla 70B 1.4T ✓ Better downstream perf

💡 Chinchilla has fewer parameters but 5× more training tokens. This is the principle behind our design.

Progressive Scaling Stages

Stage 1
1B
Validate tokenizer, optimizer, and data quality before investing more compute
Stage 2
3B
Evaluate multilingual learning and corpus mixture quality
Stage 3
8B
Verify scaling behaviour — does reasoning and coding emerge?
Stage 4
40B
Full-scale production training. Expert-level reasoning, coding, multilingual, and agentic capabilities
🎓 Progressive Curriculum — What Each Model Learns First
Model Size Primary Objective Curriculum Focus
1B Language, syntax, grammar, basic world knowledge Primarily Web Foundation + Always-ON
3B Multilingual understanding + factual knowledge Increase Web Diverse + introduce Code & STEM
8B Programming, reasoning, mathematical capabilities Higher Code + STEM emphasis
40B Expert-level reasoning, coding, agentic tasks Full mixture + curriculum-based data selection

📐 The principle: smaller models need broad linguistic competence first; larger models have the capacity to absorb specialized technical knowledge.

03 · Tokenizer Design

The Fertility Problem in Multilingual Models

Transformer models process tokens, not words. The tokenizer determines how efficiently the training budget is used. For multilingual models, poorly designed tokenizers impose a heavy fertility tax on Indic languages.

⚠️ The Fertility Tax — Why English Tokenizers Fail for Indic Languages

Using a tokenizer like GPT-3's cl100k on the same sentence across languages:

Language Tokens Used Relative Cost Visual
English 14 1×
Hindi 80 5.7×
Tamil 97 6.9×
Telugu 150 10.7×
💸 Consequence: Under a 1 trillion token budget, the model observes ~833B English words but only ~556B Telugu words — a 33% information deficit for Telugu, despite identical compute.
